\section{Creative Enhancements}

Most of these came out of something failing on real footage. They are what separates
the system from a straightforward ``chunk the video and embed the captions'' pipeline.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lL{3.6cm}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Enhancement} & \textbf{Why it matters} \\
\midrule
E-1 & Multi-signal chunking with weight redistribution &
Four detectors vote on where to cut, and a signal that never fires gives its share to
the ones that did. Without this, fixed-camera footage returns as a single chunk. \\
E-2 & Several chunkings of one video at once &
Chunk settings are stored with the results, so a video can be indexed coarsely and
finely at the same time and a search uses whichever suits it. \\
E-3 & Multiple vectors per chunk &
Description, people, actions and objects are embedded separately, so a query about
someone's appearance is matched against a short person description instead of
competing with everything else in that chunk. \\
E-4 & Cheap detectors used as gates &
YOLO and EasyOCR decide whether a frame is worth sending to the vision model. Frames
with nothing to see or read never cost anything. \\
E-5 & Detector labels hidden from the vision model &
YOLO called a thermal-imaged tank an ``airplane'' with 0.73 confidence. Showing it the
boxes without the labels, the vision model said ``tank''. \\
E-6 & Adaptive frame sampling &
Frames are kept by how different they are from the last one kept. A fixed threshold
kept one frame on a static camera; a purely relative one collected compression noise. \\
E-7 & Questions routed to summaries, not chunks &
A question is matched to the video-level output that can answer it --- entities for a
person, novelty for ``anything odd'', statistics for ``how busy'' --- rather than
searching segments and hoping. \\
E-8 & Constrained entity linking &
People are clustered on appearance under hard constraints (two people visible in the
same chunk cannot be the same person) \emph{before} an LLM writes anything. Asked to
match people directly, an LLM merges anyone in dark clothing. \\
E-9 & Selectable response detail &
The same search returns a few hundred tokens or tens of thousands. A person wants
everything; an agent pays per token. \\
E-10 & Self-describing API &
One endpoint returns every analyzer, field and filter the system supports, so an LLM
agent can discover what it can ask instead of being hard-coded against it. \\
E-11 & Content-addressed videos &
A video is identified by the hash of its bytes, so the same file uploaded twice is
recognised as the same video and never processed or stored twice. \\
E-12 & Agentic front end with clip artifacts &
Results open in a side panel as a playable clip reel, so ``show me every time someone
went behind the counter'' produces something you can watch end to end. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
